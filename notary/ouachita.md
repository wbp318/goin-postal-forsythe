# Ouachita Parish: notary commission from Monroe

For a candidate who lives in Ouachita Parish (Rin, if resident, or a staff member). The commission is issued for the parish of residence, which is the parish of voter registration. The store at 1800 Forsythe Avenue is in Ouachita Parish, so for an Ouachita resident the home parish and the work parish are the same, which keeps every filing in one courthouse.

Written 4 November 2026. Phone every office before driving; hours below come from directories, not the offices' own sites, and are marked (confirm).

## Step 0: voter registration

You must be registered to vote in Ouachita Parish on the day you file the Application to Qualify (R.S. 35:191(B)). Check first at https://voterportal.sos.la.gov. Fix the parish or address before applying.

Ouachita Parish Registrar of Voters
- Address: 701 St. John Street, Monroe, LA 71201 (the office moved here in October 2025 from 1650 Desiard Street, Suite 125)
- Phone: 318-327-1436 (confirm; this number was listed for the old Desiard Street office)

- Online: register or update at https://voterportal.sos.la.gov with a Louisiana driver's licence or ID.

## Clerk of court: where the duplicate oath goes

After passing, the oath of office is taken before a notary and two originals are signed. One goes to the Secretary of State with the bond, the signature form and the $35 commission documents fee. The duplicate goes to the clerk of court of the parish of commission within one month of the oath. Since 1 February 2026 a surety-company bond no longer has to be approved or recorded by the clerk (R.S. 35:192(C), Act 258 of 2025); only a personal-surety bond or a special-mortgage bond still goes through the clerk's mortgage office. Buy the surety-company bond and skip that.

Ouachita Parish Clerk of Court (Fourth Judicial District)
- Address: 301 South Grand Street, Suite 104, Monroe, LA 71201 (the parish courthouse, about 2 miles from the store)
- Mailing: P.O. Box 1862, Monroe, LA 71210-1862
- Phone: 318-327-1444
- Fax: 318-327-1462
- Hours: Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed weekends (confirm)
- E-recording: not available per the deeds.com listing (confirm; this matters because every act of sale or mortgage on Ouachita property that the notary passes must be filed here under R.S. 35:199)
- Fee to file the duplicate oath: ask (confirm). Clerks typically charge a small filing fee.

Ask the clerk at the same visit:

1. Whether they want the duplicate oath "filed" or "recorded" and the fee for each.
2. Whether they keep a notary register or signature card for the parish.
3. The current recording fee schedule for conveyances and mortgages, and whether they take acts by mail or require in-person drop-off. The store will be recording acts here regularly.
4. Whether UCC-1 filings are accepted at the counter.
5. Whether the clerk's office offers a notary public ex officio service (the clerk is ex officio notary under the Constitution); this is the office that currently covers what "Notary on call" sends away.

## Nearest exam site

All sittings are at LSU in Baton Rouge only (Himes Hall area, Memorial Clock Tower, accessible entrance via David Boyd Hall). From Monroe it is about 170 miles and 3 hours by car via US-165 south to I-49, or I-20 west to US-167 (confirm the route). Go down the night before; the exam is a four-hour sitting with an early check-in given in the admission email about five days ahead. Visitor parking near the Clock Tower is paid on weekdays and generally open on Saturdays. LSU Welcome Center: 225-578-5030.

## Local differences worth knowing

- Ouachita is a large parish (about 160,000 people) with a busy clerk's office. Expect lines at the recording counter; mornings mid-week are quietest (confirm).
- Everything is in one courthouse complex: the clerk at 301 South Grand, the registrar a few blocks away at 701 St. John. One trip covers Step 0 and the oath filing.
- The store's acts will almost all be recorded here. Learn the counter procedure and the fee schedule before the first customer act. A standing account with the clerk for recording fees is worth asking about.
- Monroe City Court and the Fourth Judicial District Court are where any small succession or succession filings for Ouachita decedents go; the notary only prepares papers that the law lets a notary prepare, and refers anything that needs a court pleading to an attorney.
- For a candidate who lives in West Monroe: still Ouachita Parish, same offices.
- If the candidate later moves out of Ouachita, the commission must be moved to the new parish: new voter registration, then a change-of-parish filing with the Secretary of State (R.S. 35:191(E)); no new exam.
